no Jev call made). The question (the user): the arena ran slower than realtime late; how do the calls inflate and
what cuts them. The arena is lockstep (`realtime: false`): every call holds the game, so the wall time of a game
minute is the sum of its calls' latencies.

1. **The ask-on-change stops skipping.** It compares one signature of the whole picture; with over ten actors
   something differs every second (34 quiet passes of 60 at minute 4, 0-3 from minute 20), and a change in any actor
   re-asks every option of every actor.
2. **The actors grow** from 3 to about 30, and each detachment is one more group asked about every second (open
   groups 2-4 until 23:00, 8-10 in 24:00-27:00, the dearest minutes of the game).
3. **A builder's options grow** from 6 to 20 (every building it can make, about 500 characters each with the
   building's description repeated for every builder), a group's from 22 to 27-41 (an advance for each party, a walk
   for each place).
4. **A gate past 150,000 characters goes as batches one after another**, each with the whole state
   (`jev::Client::ask`): one batch 340 ms, two 831, three 1,240 (median).

## 4. The levers, replayed on the recorded gates

The same gates with the questions a rule would not have sent removed, the batches re-cut at 150,000 characters and
the latency from §1's fit.

| variant | gate tokens | gate seconds | gate s per game minute, 24:00-28:00 |
|---|---|---|---|

| A: the batches of one gate sent at once | 87.9M | 708 | 30-31 |
| B: an actor asked on its own change | 40.3M | 573 | 28-37 |

| C: two steps (`change`/`answer` first, then the opened actors' options) | 60.0M | 973 | 46-58 |
| B + C | 41.7M | 779 | 35-42 |

B as simulated: a busy builder, lab or group is asked when its course's kind or its idleness differs from its last
ask, when an event names it, when the packet, rules or lists change, when a party appears or leaves (groups only),
and after 20 s; an idle actor and every threat each second. Builders' questions fall to 14%, labs' to 44%, groups'
to 85%. Of the moves the pick played, 31 of 194 builders', 3 of 386 labs' and 104 of 1,246 groups' fell on a second
B keeps the actor quiet (they would come at the next event or within 20 s). Not simulated: the store crossing
empty or full opening the builders, which the whole-picture signature has today (B's saving is overstated by that).

- A against the service (built 2026-09-30): the recorded gate at 25:00 (700 nouls, three batches, 1,420 ms as
  played) sent with its batches at once took 683 ms median over four calls (484-737), every answer back: about
  half, where the fit's one-batch latency predicted a third; the batches slow each other.
- C adds a round trip and saves less than B; over B it saves nothing.
- B removes no call: a threat is in sight on nearly every late second (gates 1,627 to 1,613, picks 1,437 to 1,428).
  Asking an idle actor every 5 s instead removes 79 picks and puts 184 of the labs' 386 moves on a quiet second.
- The two pick calls stay at 27-30 s a game minute late. Asking stage two for the first five worlds beside stage one
  would cover the sampled candidate 59% of the time (43% from 20:00): the candidate is spread over the list.
- The description of a building said once in the state instead of in every builder's question: roughly 45M of the
  builders' 107M characters as played, roughly 6M after B (estimated from the questions' lengths, not replayed).

After A + B the game's Jev time is about 1,130 s (1,675) and its tokens about 73M (120.7M); a late game minute holds
51-58 s of calls (88-106), half of them the picks.
