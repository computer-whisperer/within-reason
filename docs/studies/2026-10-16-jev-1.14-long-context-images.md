# Jev 1.14, long context and images: spot tests (2026-10-16)

TypeSafe shipped Jev 1.14.0 on 2026-10-14 and, with it, two opt-in headers that work on 1.13 and 1.14 alike:
`TypeSafe-Danger-Zone-Long-Context: true` (262k tokens a request and a question, against 64k / 32k) and
`TypeSafe-Danger-Zone-Images: true` (PNG or JPEG assets referenced from the state, the instructions or the criteria;
a `box` question on 1.14 that returns where something is in an image). Read from `docs.typesafe.ai` `models.md`,
`api.md`, `images.md` and `primitives/box.md` on this day; `docs/harness/jev.md` carries the facts. The alias our
client sends, `jev-latest`, still answers as `jev-1.13.0`; `jev-preview` is 1.14.0. The user: "run some spot tests
for both of those in the obvious ways that matter in this project". Two instruments, both offline:
`run/jev_long_ab.py` (new: a recorded game's calls under the header and through 1.14, against the recorded answers
and a re-sent baseline) and `run/clef_spatial.py` (the spatial battery, now sending the map pictures to Jev as
assets, with Box questions on 1.14). Spend: about $1.35 of Jev. Data: `data/jev-*-2026-10-16.*`.

## 1. Long context, on the calls the client splits today

The client (`crates/jev`) sends a request whose body passes 150,000 characters as batches of whole questions over
the same state, at once, because the service refused anything past its context. Kill-test first: the largest call
of apex-v024-carrot-sym50-opus-1 (165,675 tokens, 926 questions over 31k characters of state, sent as 4 batches
in the game) sent whole without the header is `HTTP 400 max_tokens_exceeded`; with the header it is answered.
The ceiling, that call's state repeated as `snapshots` with three of its questions: 99k tokens in 1.05 s, 198k in
2.8 s, 247k in 4.1 s, 260k in 4.6 s on 1.13 (0.93 / 2.4 / 4.1 / 4.4 s on 1.14); the answers moved by hundredths
across the copies.

## 2. Jev 1.14 against 1.13, on the same replay

Latency the same at every size (the table). Answers differ beyond the drift: nouls on the other side of 0.5 for
3.0% (the drift 1.0%), |diff| median 0.04 (0.01), picks changed on 10-12% (4.5-5.5%). Where, against 1.13 re-sent
(the drift in parentheses): `place` 107 of 1,732 = 6.2% (1.3%), `change` 8.9% (2.4%), `follow_group` 6.9%
(0.4%), `gather` 7.8% (0), `join_group` 5.4% (3.9%), `send_8_party` 5.5% (3.4%), `forbidden` 14 of 769 = 1.8%
(0.1%), `attack_party` 2.3% (0.5%), `go_spot` 1.1% (0.3%), `fight_to_spot` 1.3% (0.3%); every `build_*` 0-0.5%
on both. The `pick` choice changed 5 of 37 (1 of 37), the worlds question `w2` 6 of 70 (3 of 70). 1.14 says yes
on 10.4% of nouls against 9.3%, with a lower mean (0.233 against 0.256). On the spatial battery's text encodings
the two are the same with the facts (gap 1.00, cover 0.89 against 0.93, rally 1.00 / 1.00, route 1.00); on bare
coordinates 1.14 is lower on gap (0.83 against 1.00: two instances of twelve).

Which of the two is right is not in this data. The reviewed-moment meter (`run/jev_ab.py`'s detectors) finds no
moments on the rebuilt hands' logs (carrot-opus-1 and human-33: none), so it cannot score 1.14 either.

## What it says

1. **Long context changes nothing for the hands as they are.** No recorded state comes within half of the
   per-question limit; what passes 64k is the question count, and the client's parallel batches answer in about
   half the time of one whole request under the header (412 against 785 ms, 378 against 701) with the same
   answers (agreement at the drift). The header does not move answers on ordinary calls either. It becomes
   useful only for a design that puts much more into the state (the whole roster, a game's history, a memory):
   260k tokens answer in 4.5 s, about 17 ms a thousand tokens at that size.
2. **1.14 is a different model on our questions**, not a drift: 3% of noul flags and a tenth of picks, most on
   `place`, `change`, `follow_group`, `gather` and the forbidden mark, with builds untouched. The alias will move
   to it without any change to our code; the vendor's own advice is to send the versioned id when thresholds were
   tuned on a version (H-HANDS-SWITCH's 0.15 margin, the 0.5 flags, the forbidden mark all were). Whether 1.14 is
   better needs a game or a human-verdict set; the old moment meter no longer fits the hands.
3. **A picture of the map does not fill the spatial hole for the typed questions.** On every kind Jev with the
   picture reads about like Jev on bare coordinates, and never like Jev on code's facts (cover 0.58-0.67 against
   0.93, the rally point 0.17-0.75 against 1.00, route 0.67 against 1.00). What the Clef trial found for Clef
   holds for Jev: a drawing is one more encoding of code's facts, no better than the numbers.
4. **Box is the one new thing: a position out of Jev.** On a clean drawing it finds the uncovered stretch of a
   turret line two thirds of the time and the group's main body five times in six, in 150-180 ms, for 2,700
   tokens; the numbers in the text beside the picture break it, and its confidence says nothing. That is a
   prototype-grade answer to "where", which the typed questions could never give (K-jev-words-not-numbers); code
   can check a box against the facts it already has (does a turret of reach R at the box's centre close the gap)
   before anything acts on it.

## What this does not say

One Opus game and twenty calls of one human game for the replay; the battery is synthetic (12 instances a cell,
a difference under 0.15 is noise); nothing played in the engine; no human verdict beside the 1.13 / 1.14
disagreements; the images are "dreamer-ready" by the vendor's own word (format and accuracy may change between
releases); the vendor's image-token guidance is not published, the 2,700 is measured on one picture size.
