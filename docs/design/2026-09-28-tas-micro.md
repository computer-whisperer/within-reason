# Target design: a hand-built TAS of one engagement, and the micro rules it names

**The question (the user, 2026-09-28).** "The next level of this is probably learning to micro better. One useful
exercise may be sending an opus subagent after a typical engagement where we are trying to break a position with our
typical unit mix. If Opus can build a TAS that defeats the units reliably, then that can show you what the critical
missing actions are. There are obvious shortcomings that I see in each engagement we take, but I don't have a general
set of rules that we should bake into our micro engine." A trained model for this comes later, if at all; one worked
example by hand first, to learn what kind of gap it is (decisions or execution) and to derive the rules from the diff.

**Status (2026-09-28).** Design written; nothing built. Branch `tas-micro`, worktree `../bar_bots-tas`.

    against three Centurions (armwar) and two light turrets (armllt), enemies from the truth file. Live: our value
    left 0.287 at 30 s and 60 s, theirs 0.650 then 1.0 (the turrets and Centurions repaired or replaced). The
    review of that game (`run/matches/1790550188-player-14-gecko-opening/00/review.md`) counts 1,430 of ours on the
    spot against 1,026 of soldiers and 170 of turrets, 1,100 lost for 0.
  - `2026-09-28-player-15-f3-950.json`: player-15 at 9:50, F3 (5059, 2650): seven Blitzes, four Stouts and a Rover
    against eight Pawns (the truth at the cut; the player's note says four Rocketeers under a light turret took
    the fight, so the cut may need `--radius 1200` or `--at` to hold them: check first). Live: ours 0.576 / 0.511,
    theirs 1.017 / 0.927.
- `target/release/duel --scenario FILE [--reps N] [--parallel N] [--time-limit S] [--lane X[/Y]] [--speed N]
  [--label TEXT] [--base-port N]` (`crates/arena/src/bin/duel/`: `scenario.rs`, `director.rs`, `script.rs`) spawns
  both sides as written (health by hurting after spawning, facing by a short move), gives each side its first orders,
  and fights it under the duel director: our side attack-moves (the director's plain order) with the micro lane on

- The micro crate (`crates/micro`, 10 Hz): H-MICRO-FORM, -FORM-FLANK, -FLEE, -FOCUS, -KITE, -FAN, -SPREAD, -HUNT,
  -LANE, -ROVE (`docs/heuristics.md`); `run/fire.py` (muzzled share, friendly fire), `run/micro_ledger.py`,
  `run/duel_ab.py` (a rule on and off over a batch), `run/render_scene.py` (a scene as SVG).

## The target

1. **A timed order script for our side.** `duel --scenario FILE --script FILE`: the script is JSON rows
   `{"t": seconds from the spawn, "unit": index into our side's units (or a list), "cmd": "move"|"fight"|"attack"|
   "stop"|"guard", "x", "z" | "target": index into their side's units}`; the director issues each row at its second
   (the engine takes an order a frame; 30 frames a second, the director's tick is what it is: say it in the doc).
   Rows at the same second go out in order. A row may name several units. The script replaces the director's
   attack-move for our side; the micro lane is off for our side under a script (the TAS is the whole control), on
   or off for theirs as `--lane` says.
2. **The other side's control.** `--theirs ai|track|director`: `director` is today's attack-move; `track` walks the
   positions the truth file recorded for the 60 s after the cut (`engagement.py --after` already reads them: write
   them into the scenario as `sides[1].track` rows `{"t", "unit", "x", "z"}`, issued as move orders; a unit that
   died in the record dies when it dies in the engine, not by the clock); `ai` puts BARb (the arena's profile, default
   `hard_aggressive`) on their side, if the duel's script can host an AI for one side (the arena's `script.rs` does:
   see `crates/arena/src/script.rs` and `autohost.rs` for how the AI slot is written). `track` first: it is the
   recorded opponent, and it is deterministic. `ai` second, for reliability against a reacting enemy.
3. **The judge.** Each run reports what the duel reports (the margin: value left ours minus theirs, the exchange,
   muzzled share, friendly fire, seconds) plus, for a script: the per-second position of every unit of ours and
   theirs, health, and the shots (the duel's `fire.rs` already keeps shots). `--seeds a,b,c` (or `--seed-base` with
   `--reps`) so "reliable" means the margin over eight reps on three seeds, not eight reps of one.
4. **The sight constraint.** The TAS author plans from what our units could see: the record's `en` rows (sight) at
   the cut and in the 60 s after, not the truth. `engagement.py --enemies sight --after 60` gives the sight file; the
   engine spawns the truth. A rule extracted from the script must also survive a delay of three ticks (0.3 s) before
   its order goes out: the duel's `--script-delay 0.3` shifts every row.
5. **The TAS loop.** An Opus agent (this worktree, its own duels) starts from the record's own orders (the scenario
   carries them), reads the judge's output, and revises the script until the E3 position falls with our value left
   above theirs on every seed. It writes what it tried and why in `docs/studies/2026-09-28-tas-e3.md` as it goes:
   every script version, the margin table, the reason for the change. If no script wins with fifteen Blitzes, that
   is the finding: the position needs a different mix or approach, and the study says which (a Shellshocker pair,
   a flank, waiting for the turret's repair to be spent), tried the same way.
6. **The diff.** `run/tas_diff.py <scenario> <script> <duel batch under our micro>`: the same scenario fought under
   our micro (`--lane on`, the director's attack-move) with the per-tick orders the micro issued logged (the duel
   director records what it and the lane order: add that log if it is not there), set beside the script's orders by
   second and unit: where they part (a target chosen, a unit pulled back, a flank taken, a formation held), with the
   scene at that second (`render_scene.py`). Each divergence is a candidate rule with a name, the scene, and the
   margin it is worth (the script with that row removed, over the seeds).
7. **The rules.** Each candidate rule goes into `crates/micro` as an H-MICRO-* row with a claim in
   `docs/knowledge/army.md` (or `mechanics.md`), and is judged by `run/duel_ab.py` on the two scenarios with the rule
   on and off (the seeds again), then in one live arena game read with `run/fire.py` and `run/micro_ledger.py`.
   The main session runs the live game; this worktree does everything up to it.

## Order of work (each ends with a commit here and a line in the status above)

1. `--script` and `--theirs track` in the duel, with the per-second log; the E3 scenario fought under the record's own
   orders as a script and under the director, eight reps, the margins side by side (they should match the live
   0.287 / 0.650 within the phase-2 spread, or the doc says why not).
2. The TAS on E3 by the Opus agent with the sight file and `--script-delay 0.3`, the study page growing as it goes.
3. `--theirs ai` and the winning script against BARb over three seeds.
4. `tas_diff.py` and the candidate rules, ranked by margin.
5. The rules built in `crates/micro`, `duel_ab.py` on both scenarios; a note to the main session for the live game.

## Constraints

- This worktree only: `../bar_bots-tas`, branch `tas-micro`. Its `run/data` and `run/engines` are hard-linked copies
  of the main tree's (no symlinks: a symlink at an ignored path was committed once and a merge deleted every recorded
  game; never `git add -A`, never `git add run/data` or `run/engines`; `run/scenarios/` is tracked on purpose).
- Duels use `--base-port` from 9500 to 9600 (the main tree's games run on 9300-9480, its arena on 9100/9200, the
  user's viewer on 1234, the main session's viewer on 8137). `--speed 50`, `--parallel` at most 2.
- Never kill the engine or the arena; never `pkill -f` or `pgrep -f`; kill only PIDs this worktree's commands started.
- Never touch `~/.local/share/BeyondAllReason`; never run `run/install_ai.sh` or `run/install_to_bar.sh` here; never
  launch the arena here. `cargo build --release` in this worktree's own `target/` (if the tool policy refuses it,
  `CARGO_TARGET_DIR=<scratchpad>/target-tas`).
- The main tree's `run/matches` is read-only from here; this worktree's duel batches go to its own `run/matches`.
- No model calls from code; the agent is the TAS author. No API keys are needed.
- Reports match reality: every margin quoted comes from a duels.csv this worktree wrote, with its batch path.
