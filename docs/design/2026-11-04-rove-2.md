# Rove 2: cover, healing, the push and the needle (proposal, 2026-11-04)

The user, 2026-11-04: "let's update the rove system. It has been quite successful so far, but we should be able to
extend it. Here are a few things I would like to include: using a mex as cover from the guarding llt (block line of
sight), returning to some repair source to heal before continuing, pushing into light resistance when success is
expected, and carefully threading the needle between enemy static defenses to reach undefended targets."

A proposal. Nothing here is built.

## 1. What roves today

`crates/micro/src/rove.rs` (H-MICRO-ROVE), the micro engine's, so under the engine's exception to the no-live-heuristics
ruling. Its hosts: the flagship's hands (a group picked `rove` or `rove_1`, with or without a marked region,
`brain/micro.rs` declares it `GroupKind::Rove`) and the duel director (`duel/director.rs`, a scenario's `rove` script
row). The distilled bot's raiders (D-RAIDERS, `bots/distilled/src/army.rs`) are their own rule and do not use it; the
territory bot has nothing roving. So this is a flagship change (and a drill harness change), measured in the
flagship's games.

Ten times a second, per rover, in this order, the first rung that applies wins:

1. **Evade.** Every armed thing (in sight, remembered buildings, soldiers seen within 10 s) is kept at its reach plus a
   margin: 1.5 s of a mobile shooter's speed (at least 80), 60 for a building. Standing inside one, or inside one in
   half a second of its velocity, the rover steps to the least threatened cell within 200.
2. **Kill what is unguarded.** The best unarmed ground unit within 1,000 that no margin reaches, whose *straight* way
   from the rover crosses no margin, and that the rover alone kills within 45 s: a walking constructor first, then an
   extractor, a radar, anything else. One `Attack` by id; the engine walks the rover to wherever it first has the
   target in range.
3. **Look.** The least-known place (his perimeter stale by the minute, then the map's unseen spots, then the stalest),
   from six tenths of its sight short of it, by a straight way crossing no margin; else stand.

Everything is per rover: two rovers only avoid taking the same target or the same place. The way test is one straight
segment against discs (`clear_way`). Health is read only to notice unseen fire. Nothing of his is ever fought.

The four asks are four new rungs and one changed primitive (the way). Below, each with the engine fact it rests on,
its rule, and the drill that kills it before any game.

## 2. The stand: attacking from a chosen point

Two of the asks (cover, and a milder cousin the user did not name) come down to one change: the attack rung
chooses **where the rover stands to fire**, not only what it fires at. Today the target is judged by *its* position
("no margin reaches the target") and the engine picks the firing spot, which is the first in-range point on the
rover's side, so a mex 350 from a light laser turret is left alone though a Rover could kill it from 170 on the
far side, 520 from the turret and 90 outside its reach.

The rule: a target is attackable when a **stand** exists: a point within the rover's reach of the target (`reach -
STAND_SLACK`, 20), on the rover's ground, outside every margin, with a way to it (§5). The stand is chosen on the
far side of the target from the nearest shooter, as far from the target as the reach allows (but see the falloff
below). A rover that drifts off its stand by more than `STAND_DRIFT` (24) is sent again.

**Firing is never an order of its own.** The user, on the first draft: "we should also not stop attacking a structure
on the way in or out of its range; the formation system we built shows we can cleanly set units to attack
asynchronously with movement." The group's contact design already rests on that (`2026-10-28-contact-cleanup.md`
§5: a Move never diverts, the weapons fire at will on the move). Today's rover sends `Attack` by id, which the
engine's walk-then-fire owns, and every evade step is a `Move` that cancels it, so the rover stops shooting on the
way out. After: the rover is only ever sent `Move`s (the way in, the stand, the step out), and its target is **set**
beside them with BAR's target-on-the-move command (`luarules/gadgets/unit_target_on_the_move.lua`,
`GameCMD.UNIT_SET_TARGET`: the unit fires at that target whenever it can while doing whatever else it was told; the
companion cancel command clears it). The shim has no custom-command path yet: a new `Command::SetTarget { unit,
target }` and `Command::ClearTarget { unit }` in `bot-protocol`, sent by `ai-host` as `COMMAND_UNIT_CUSTOM` with the
gadget's id and the target as the parameter. A shared-crate change, so both regimes get it; the distilled bot's
army could use it later. The target stays set through an evade step and is cleared when the target dies or the
rover gives it up, so the rover shoots on the way in (from the moment the target is in reach), while it stands,
and on the way out, and the stand is only where it stops. The engine still fires at will at anything else in
reach when the set target is not, as the groups' members do.

**Laser falloff.** The engine fact (the user: "the damage dropoff for lasers can definitely be part of consideration
here"): a beam laser's hit does `max(minIntensity, 1 - distance / (2 × range))` of its damage
(`BeamLaser.cpp`), and BAR sets no `minIntensity`, so every laser does half damage at the edge of its reach. Both
sides here are lasers. An LLT's 160 a second at the muzzle is 91 at 370 and 80 at 430; a Rover's 35 a second is 18
at 170 of its 180. So a Rover standing at the LLT's edge lives twice as long as the muzzle number says (1.3 s, not
0.66), and a Rover killing a mex from its own full reach takes twice as long (15 s against 8 from close in). The
stand's choice is a trade: the first cut takes every shooter's damage at the stand's distance (the margin stays by
reach, the *odds* and the kill time by the falloff), prefers the stand that kills soonest among those outside every
margin, and only when none is outside a margin (the cover case) takes the far one. The falloff belongs in the
lane's `Source` as a function of distance, not a constant, and the push's odds (§4) need the simulator to know it
(to check: `combatsim` models reach and damage a second; whether it scales by distance is to read before the push
is built).

The rim stand alone opens every mex more than about 320 from an LLT and more than about 150 from a Pawn's standing
place. The cover stand is the same machinery with one more constraint.

### 2.1 Cover: the mex as a shield

The engine fact: every weapon has `avoidFriendly`, default true in Recoil (`WeaponDef.cpp`: "does the weapon
avoid shooting if there's allies in the way"), and the light laser turret's def sets nothing against it
(`armllt.lua`, `corllt.lua`). `CWeapon::HaveFreeLineOfFire` traces the shot from the aim-from position to the
target and refuses it when an ally's collision volume lies in the cone. So an LLT whose own extractor stands
between it and a Rover does not fire; it retargets if it can, else holds. The extractor's collision volume is a
cylinder 48 wide and 33 tall (`armmex.lua`), a Rover is 21 wide and 16 tall, the turret 77 tall with its muzzle
somewhere in its top half. Whether the beam clears the extractor's top depends on the muzzle height and the
distances; the drill decides it, not this document (§7, drill C0).

The rule: when no rim stand exists because the target itself is inside a shooter's reach, and that shooter is one
static shooter of a **coverable** kind (`COVER_KINDS`: the light laser turrets by name, `armllt`, `corllt`,
`legllt`, until a drill admits another), the stand is the **shadow point**: on the ray from the shooter through the
target's centre, beyond the target, within the rover's reach of it and as far along the ray as the reach allows
(the widest shadow and the shortest exit), outside every *other* margin, on the ground, with a way (§5) that
enters the covered shooter's margin only along the shadow (the last leg comes in from behind the target, never
across the open). While the rover stands in the shadow the covered shooter is exempt from the evade rung for that
rover; the moment the target dies, or the rover is off the shadow line by more than half the target's width minus
its own (`SHADOW_TOLERANCE`, about 13 at the target, more further back), the exemption ends and the evade rung
fires as today.

The risk, in numbers: the LLT does 75 every 0.467 s, 160 a second, and a Rover has 105 health: 0.66 s to die.
A Rover standing 170 behind a mex that is 200 from the LLT is 370 from the LLT and needs 60 elmos (0.36 s plus
the turn) to leave its reach once the mex dies; the turret's retarget is its next slow update. The drill reads the
survival; if the exit loses the rover, the shadow stand is restricted to targets whose stand lies within
`COVER_EXIT` of the reach's edge (so the exit is a lunge, not a run), which still buys the band between 150 and
320 from an LLT.

A target covered by two shooters, or by a mobile one (which walks round the cover), has no shadow stand. The
shadow is also where a Rover would stand to kill the LLT's own frame being built, which today's rule attacks
anyway (a frame guards nothing).

**Armed extractors are shooters, not cover** (the user: "don't forget exploiters exist"). Cortex's Exploiter
(`corexp`: 240 metal, 1,440 health, a 435 laser at 160 a second, an extractor at the standard rate), its tier-2
fortified extractor (`cormexp`, missiles and a 650 laser) and Legion's armed extractor (`legmext15`) have weapons,
so the lane already lists them as sources and never as targets (`weapon_count > 0`), and the cover rule takes only an
unarmed structure as the thing to hide behind, which the target rule's own class test guarantees. What changes
with them on the field: an Exploiter guards its own spot and every spot within 435, so a field of his with one
Exploiter has rim stands only beyond 320 of it and no shadow stand at all; and the attack rung's "extractor"
class must never be read off the name or the extraction rate, only off the weapon count, as today.

## 3. Heal: back to a builder before going on

The user, on the first draft: "we should definitely find a way to add constructors and the commander as heal
sources soon. The first few rovers out of the factory hit very hard because of how dear each mex is to the enemy,
and the few seconds of build time necessary to heal one is probably worth it. It should be something the player
can disable however (either a disableable micro engine behavior, or a jev menu option -- something to design
carefully in any case)."

The engine fact: an idle builder does nothing on its own (`BuilderCAI.cpp`, `SlowUpdate`: an empty queue returns).
The flagship's `nanos.rs` gives every construction turret the UI's fight order, under which the builder fight loop
"repairs or helps build whatever of ours stands within its build distance", so a nano heals a rover that parks in
reach with nothing said to it. A constructor or the commander heals only under a `Repair` order, which is a hands
actor's order. The rates: a Rover's 105 health over its 1,100 build time is 19 a second from a nano (200 build
power), 29 from the commander (300) and 7.6 from a constructor (80); from half health that is 3, 2 and 7 seconds of
the builder's time, plus the rover's walk. Against that, a Rover is 31 metal and his mex 50 plus the spot, and
the first Rovers' kills are what hold his economy down (the Apex comparison of 2026-11-03: the flagship's first
dozen Blitzes at his side held him to 5-8 extractors to 9:00).

**Two halves, one switch.** The rover's half is the lane's: where it walks and when. The builder's half is the
hands': the `Repair`. Of the user's two placements, the switch goes on the rover's half, because with the rover
never walking there is nothing for a builder to do, and the hands then need no switch of their own:

- `Footwork` gains `heal: bool`, on by default, set by the player's `lane` tool per group or for `all`
  (`lane group_R1: heal off`), the same shape as `flee`, `fan` and `kite` (H-HANDS-LANE), and the brief's lane
  paragraph says what it does in a line. The lane tool is footwork, and where a hurt rover walks is footwork; the
  bypass the user closed in 2026-10-07 was a unit-control *task* set by the lane, which this is not. A Jev menu
  move was the alternative: it would cost a gate and the ask cadence per heal, and the hands would still need the
  Repair. (If the user prefers the builder's time to be Jev's call, the hands' half below becomes a menu move
  `heal_<group>` on the builder, offered while a hurt rover stands in its reach.)
- The rover's rule: with no target in hand and no stand held, below `HEAL_BELOW` (0.5 of its max health), it picks
  the **healer** that gets it back to roving soonest: the walk (by the needle, §5) plus the heal time at the
  builder's power, over the tick's own builders that are a nano, a constructor or the commander, the commander and
  constructors only within `HEAL_WALK` (2,000) so a rover deep in his half comes home to a nano rather than pulling
  a builder's attention across the map; it walks to a point inside that builder's build distance less `HEAL_SLACK`
  (30), on the side away from the nearest shooter, stands there (`doing: "healing"`, the event `Heals { at }`
  naming the builder) until `HEAL_UNTIL` (0.9), or `HEAL_PATIENCE` (20 s) without its health rising, or a threat
  (the evade rung stays above). Its goal is kept across the heal. A hurt rover with a target in hand finishes it.
- The hands' half: a builder of the flagship's with a hurt rover standing in its reach that named it (the `Heals`
  event, kept by `groups.rs` while the rover stands) is sent `Repair` on that rover ahead of its current step, once
  a second while the rover stands there (the shape of `nanos.rs`'s frames and H-ECO-NANO-GUARD), and its list
  resumes when the rover leaves: the hands re-send states every second already. **The builder never moves** (the
  user, 2026-11-04: "I picture the rover running back to the nearest heal-capable unit to receive its healing:
  meaning the constructors never have to waste time moving"): the Repair is given only once the rover stands inside
  the builder's build distance, so the engine has nothing to walk to, and a rover that stands outside it (the
  builder walked on with its list, the stand snapped elsewhere) gets no Repair and follows the builder by its own
  rule, re-reading the heal point each tick. A code decision to enter in `docs/code-decisions.md` (a builder's
  seconds, bounded by the rates above, spent on the lane's word). Nanos need nothing. A duel scenario has no hands,
  so drill H scripts the Repair.
- The commander: the same rule, with one more guard: never while it fights or is itself hurt below `HEAL_BELOW`
  (H-HANDS-COMMANDER-FIGHTS outranks it).

Why 0.5: the margins keep a healthy rover unhit; health is lost to unseen fire and to the exits above, and a Rover
under half cannot take one LLT shot at the muzzle (75) or two Grunt shots (37 each) on the way out of anything.

## 4. The push: the pack against light resistance

The engine knows nothing here; the question is the odds, and the odds are the host's: the flagship has the combat
simulator (`brain/combat.rs` `odds(ours, theirs)`, `combatsim::sim::odds` under it) and the hunt's offer already
rests on it. The lane stays free of the simulator: `View` gains
`fn odds(&self, ours: &[(UnitDefId, f32)], theirs: &[(UnitDefId, f32)]) -> Option<f32>` (types with their health),
`None` by default (a host without a simulator pushes nothing).

The rule: rovers of one declared group within `PACK_RADIUS` (400) of each other are a **pack**. For a target of the
usual classes within `ATTACK_RADIUS` that has neither a rim nor a shadow stand, the pack's **cover set** is every
shooter whose margin covers the target or the pack's way to it. The push is offered when every member of the cover
set is in sight (no pushing a radar contact), and the host's odds for the pack against the cover set plus the target
are at least `PUSH_ODDS` (0.8), and the metal the odds expect the pack to lose is
under the metal of the cover set and the target together (a trade, not a sacrifice; the expected loss is
`(1 - odds) × the pack's metal`, the crude first cut, the simulator's own loss figure if `Odds` carries one). The
pack then attacks the cover set first, nearest shooter first, every member on the same target, and the target
after; while the push stands every shooter of the cover set is exempt from the evade rung for the pack's members,
and the odds are read again every `PUSH_REREAD` (1 s): a new shooter in the set, a member lost, or the odds
under `PUSH_BREAK` (0.6) ends the push, the pack evades as today, and the target is blocked for `BLOCK_FRAMES`.

The worked case: four Rovers (31 metal, 105 health, 35 a second each) on a Grunt (43 metal, 280 health, 74 a second,
reach 215, speed 81) escorting a constructor: 140 a second kills the Grunt in 2 s, the Grunt kills one Rover in
1.4 s; the trade is 31 for 43 plus the constructor, and the simulator will say about 0.9. One Rover against the
same Grunt is 0; the rule never fires for a lone rover against anything armed, which is what a single Rover should
do. A Pawn escort (370 health, 87 a second) against four Rovers: 2.6 s to kill, one Rover lost; about the same
trade. Two escorts: the simulator decides, and the metal bar holds it back. A building is not excluded by rule (the
first draft barred them): an LLT against four Rovers is a bad trade the simulator sees (it kills two before it
falls), an Exploiter (240 metal, 1,440 health) against ten Rovers at its edge (80 a second there) is a fair one, and
the metal bar decides both.

## 5. The needle: the way as a route, not a segment

Today `clear_way` tests one straight segment against the margins, and a place whose straight way crosses one is
given up for 30 s (or never taken), so two LLTs 1,100 apart with a mex field behind them wall the rover off though
the gap between their margins is 120 wide (not enough) and at 1,300 apart it is 320 (plenty). The lane already
owns the ground: `terrain::Field::over_until` (a priced Dijkstra field from origins, stopped at a point) and
`Field::route`, and `ground.rs` builds each class's passable grid and prices cells by their clearance.

The rule: a **way** from the rover to a point is the route over the rover's class costs with every cell inside a
margin **closed**, and cells within `NEEDLE_SLACK` (100) outside a margin priced up (so the route walks the middle
of a gap, not its lip), and the clearance priced for the rover's half-width plus a cell (a gap narrower than the
rover plus the engine's jitter is closed by the ground itself). The route is straightened by line of sight as the
group's legs are, then cut into waypoints no more than `NEEDLE_LEG` (320, two Rover-seconds) apart, sent as one
queued chain of `Move`s. The way is planned when a goal or stand is chosen, when a shooter newly covers a leg
(checked every tick against the current chain, which is cheap), and every `NEEDLE_REPLAN` (5 s) at most; a field
bounded by `over_until` on the 16-elmo grid costs a few milliseconds, well inside the lane's thread (one plan a
second across six rovers at the most). `clear_way` goes, and every caller (the attack stand, the look point, the
heal point, the give-up "its way passes the reach") reads "has a way" instead. The evade rung's step is unchanged:
a step is not a way.

The backstop stays: between waypoints the engine's own pathfinder can swing wide round a wall into a margin, and
the evade rung catches it where it stands, as today. Short legs keep that rare.

## 6. The rungs after the change

Per rover, each tick, first that applies:

1. Evade (shooters exempted by a held shadow stand or a standing push excepted). The set target stays set: the
   rover shoots on the way out.
2. Walk to or hold a stand, or push, with the target set.
3. Heal (hurt, nothing held; `Footwork::heal` on).
4. Take a target: open (as now), else a rim stand, else a shadow stand, else a push the pack's odds allow.
5. Look, by the needle; else stand.

Words to the host: `RoveWhat::Attacks` gains `from: Open | Rim | Cover | Push`, two new events `Heals { at }` and
`Pushes { against, target }`, and `RoveReport::doing` gains "healing", "pushing" and "threading" (on a way with more
than one leg). `groups.rs` `rove_events` and `picture.rs` turn them into the report's lines as they do the four
today. The `lane` tool gains `heal`; the brief's lane paragraph a line. The pick is still `rove`.

## 7. Drills, before any game

One scenario each under the duel director, in `docs/data/scenarios-2026-11-04/`, as the air-rove drill is laid
out (a scenario JSON with the two sides, a script JSON with `"cmd": "rove"` at 0.0). Each is a kill test in the
sense of the user's rule: a crisp expected outcome, the piece withdrawn when it fails.

- **T (the set target).** One Rover sent a `Move` past a cormex with the target set on it: hits on the mex logged
  while the Rover is moving, before and after it passes, and none after the clear. The shim fact, before anything
  rests on it.
- **R (rim).** One Rover; a cormex 350 from a corllt. Today: the mex is left (guarded). After: the mex dead, the Rover
  never hit, its first hit on the mex landing before it stands.
- **C0 (the cover fact).** One Rover held (the scenario's hold-fire prep), standing on the shadow line 170 behind a
  cormex 200 from a corllt, for 20 s; then the same 40 off the line. Expected: no damage event on the first, death
  on the second. This is the engine fact and the height geometry in one; if the beam clears the mex, §2.1 is dropped
  and only the rim stand stays.
- **C (cover).** One Rover roving; cormex 200 from corllt. Expected: the mex dead; read the Rover's health at the exit
  and whether it lives. The survival sets `COVER_EXIT` or kills the piece.
- **H (heal).** One Rover at 25 health; a nano of ours under the UI's fight order (a script row) 1,500 behind it; a
  cormex 2,000 ahead. Expected: the Rover walks to the nano first, leaves at 90 or more, kills the mex. Then the
  same with a constructor in the nano's place and a scripted `Repair` when the rover arrives (the hands' half,
  which the director has not); then with `heal off`: the Rover goes straight for the mex.
- **P (push).** Four Rovers; a corck constructor with one corak beside it. Expected: the Grunt attacked by all four,
  then the constructor, at most one Rover lost. Then the same with a corllt in place of the Grunt: no push, the
  constructor left. The director needs a `View::odds`; the arena has `combatsim` for it, else a scripted answer.
- **N (needle).** Two corllts 1,300 apart on a line, a cormex 600 behind the line's middle, the Rover 1,200 before it
  and 500 off the middle, so its straight way crosses one margin. Expected: the mex dead, the Rover never inside a
  reach. Then the turrets 1,100 apart: the mex left alone, no attempt.

Then one flagship game on the arena target (`--matches 1`, the player; Apex v0.5.04 cold, Comet), read by the rove
lines in the record and the group's `rove` log: rovers lost, what they killed and from which stand, heals, and the
player's reading of the new words. The game is the measure of whether the pieces matter; the drills are the measure
of whether they work.
