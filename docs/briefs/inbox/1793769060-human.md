HANDS AND LATENCY — THE REAL CONSTRAINT
This is where I lost most of my value, and the next player should read this twice.

Orders landed in blocks on the half-minute, 30 s late routinely and once 95 s late (my 13:10 orders landed at 14:45). Assume every instruction you write takes effect a minute later and write instructions that are still right a minute from now. Never send a correction you cannot afford to have arrive late: I ordered a plant reclaim during a seat correction, cancelled it, and the cancel lost the race — the plant died at 3:41. Same with a dropped plant frame at 4:35: my drop arrived 20 s late when the frame was already 80%, so the sane move was to finish it for 116 metal.

The hands only move units to places that are on their menu. Named waypoints I invented (north_gather, G7, spot_28) were simply not read, and groups sat still for minutes — group_D idled at I7 until I restated the way as plain cell letters (D4, C4, B5, A4) and marked front_g7. Restate destinations as cells, and check the next turn that the group actually moved; "ordered" is not "moving."

The hands rate targets and will refuse yours. My Valiants rated the Tyrannus at 0.05 and would not attack it twice, even restated with it first in the packet; I had to move them over the cell instead and let contact happen. Similarly you cannot split air by type: 9 new Whirlwinds sat inside the Valiant group and three Valiants could not be sent alone. If you want bombers used separately, build them into their own group from the plant, counted, from the start.

Name units in your own faction's words. My first constructor's list was refused because I wrote an Armada turret name while playing Cortex; a single bad step (a bad moho step) refused a whole queue call. Keep lists short so one bad step costs little, and the commander cannot place vehicle-plant structures (coravp) — a constructor must.
