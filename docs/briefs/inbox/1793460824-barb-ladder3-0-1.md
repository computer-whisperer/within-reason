We won in 19.4 minutes from corner W (commander at B8, 1286/5421) against a bot-lab opening. Here is what mattered, in order.

The opening line held: 2 extractors, 2 solars, vehicle plant facing east, 3rd extractor, then the commander helping the plant at low priority with solars slotted in when energy ran dry. Plant list: Rover, con, 2 Rovers, con, then Blitzes and cons interleaved. Three Rovers in his half by 1:55 found his base at G1 (6800,752) — north-east corner, extractors and solars there, and a Tick at our home by 2:13 meant a bot lab. Expect that; it is probably the same every game from this corner.

The Tick cost us the most of anything early: two extractors, a half-built solar, about a minute of expansion, and twenty seconds of the commander chasing something it can never catch. Do not chase. Build a light turret at home immediately (about 8 s) and put one Rover in its own group at home — the Rover is fast enough to catch a Tick, the commander and Blitzes are not. Better: have the turret standing before 2:10. Have constructors pair each new extractor with a light turret in the middle; that is what finally stopped the Pawn nibbling.

Economy: 4 extractors at 3:20 and 7 at 4:40 is 40 s behind the strong players' pace, and the Tick is why. Energy piled up unused early while metal was the limit — stop solars and have the commander take spots itself once that happens. Later the reverse: by 18:17 energy was 12 s from zero at 1,120 made against 1,855 spent across four plants plus assisting builders, and empty energy stops the extractors too. Pull builders off plants onto energy before it bites.

The biggest and most expensive lesson is how the army walks. For about three minutes (11:30–14:50) the army existed as a dozen to nine pieces that each arrived separately and got eaten one at a time by a 900-metal party of Rocketeers and Centurions, while a 5,600-metal advantage did nothing. Three separate causes, all needing explicit instruction: (1) plants sending new soldiers directly into the main group makes the group span home to the front, so its position reads as home and it walks as one long string — have plants make fresh groups that follow and then join; (2) the group split into nine companies each marching its own way, with the "main body" being whichever company was largest and sometimes halted at home to shoot something 3,700 away — set company to 0 on the main group so it is one formation; (3) say that the joined body fights to the objective together. Do this at the start, not at 14:00.

His Gauntlet turret at F3 covers 1,220 and the F2–F3 nest. Attacking into it frontally traded roughly even, which is a loss when you are four to one ahead: at 12:52 we lost 2,287 to kill ~1,800 with 10 of 35 soldiers in the fight at a time. Do not stand under it and do not grind the nest. The route that worked, every stop over 1,220 from the Gauntlet: E5, F5, G4 ×2, H3 ×2, H2, then into G1 from the east. G4 and H3 are his thinly-guarded extractors on the way. Note our own units read G4 as inside cover even at ~1,500; ignore that reading.

Rally outside reach when gathering (D3 worked). Beamers, Sentries and an Overwatch in and around his base chip a unit or two at a time from out of sight — that is survivable at four to one, but it means arriving as one body and killing turrets deliberately rather than loitering.

Timings to beat: his first tier-2 unit (a Gunslinger, then Hounds at 650 reach) at 17:04 from an advanced bot lab at G2. Hounds and Gunslingers eat Blitzes in the open; we switched plants to Stouts plus a Janus. His advanced lab is due around 11:00 by the usual curve, so our window with Blitzes is 4–7 minutes and the lead should be converted well before 17:00 — we were 7,200 to 750 at 11:26 and still took eight more minutes. Our advanced vehicle plant went down at 14:53 at home with four constructors plus the commander, once metal and energy balanced and plants were idling; six Bulls ordered at 17:14.

Last thing, and it nearly cost the game: at 19:07 his commander stood in his base beside our 46-soldier group and I sent everything at it from all sides. The group arrived strung out and his D-gun killed 10 Stouts and 4 Blitzes — 18 units, 3,475 metal — in one pass. It did kill the advanced bot lab and two advanced constructors. Do not rush a commander with a long thin column. Bring one formed body, ideally with Bulls, and accept a few seconds' delay to form up; the D-gun pierces a file.
