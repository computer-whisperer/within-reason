We timed out at 31.9 minutes with 19k metal banked, an army rated roughly 8-to-1 over everything we could see, and his base still standing. The economy opening on this map against this bot is solved; converting a huge lead into a kill is not. Read this as a warning about the second half, not the first.

What worked, and I would repeat verbatim:

Opening pool line from B8 (corner W): extractor, extractor, solar, solar, vehicle plant, third extractor, then the commander helps the plant at low priority, then two more solars. Energy stalls at 1:30 in every game here (70 in against 217 draw) — plan the two extra solars for ~1:40 rather than reacting to the stall. Plant order: Rover, constructor, 2 Rovers, constructor, then Blitzes with a constructor every three. Put the plant's output on by_type so Blitzes don't rove off with the scouting Rovers. We were at 6 extractors by 3:16 and 9 by 4:37, both above the strong players' upper quartile, and 15 extractors / 36 metal a second by 7:15.

Keep the commander out of the plant's exit lane — I lost 18 seconds of plant output at 1:30 to exactly that. The same bug family bit me four more times later (nanos and extractors placed in lab exit lanes at 10:47, 18:39, 23:53, 30:20); check every constructor's placement near a factory mouth.

Sentries beside the home extractors before his first raider (~2:10) are mandatory. I was late at spot_73 and lost that extractor to a single Tick at 2:26, which then went on to shoot a constructor and the plant. Don't send the commander 1,100+ away to place a turret while something is chewing on the plant — turret at home first, then the far spot.

His factory was a bot lab at H2 (7200,800), base H1/H2, as in the prior games here. That means Pawns. Blitzes beat Pawns about 2:1 in the open, and that window is 4-7 minutes — but Pawn swarms outrun tanks, and from 10 minutes on they killed middle spots all game while our tank body could never catch them. Decide early that Pawns are not the main body's business and say so in the packet; I only wrote that at 16:46 after a group spent 40 seconds oscillating between three spots chasing them.

Start the advanced vehicle plant earlier than I did. I began at 7:15 and it stood at 9:44 (the previous game: 11:27). Even that was too late to matter, because the thing that actually capped us from 10:00 onward was energy, not metal: metal sat at cap (2.9k, then 13k, then 19.4k) while energy balance was flat. If you are winning the expansion race here, commit to fusion plus a large solar block by about 8:00 and keep adding; otherwise you bank metal you cannot spend and the game drifts to a timeout, which is what happened to me.

The failure, stated plainly: his F3 nest (Pulsar, Gauntlet, Beamer, Overwatch, Sentries) ate our army in trickles for twenty minutes. Between 29:30 and 30:10 we lost about 8,400 metal a minute into it while killing a third of that. The causes were (a) the main army arriving in pieces instead of as one body — at 9:57 it was fragmented into six groups under his F4 Sentries and lost 3 Blitzes in a minute — and (b) the automatic step-back from turret reach, which kept pulling the merged body out of the Pulsar's range so it stood at E5 for 15-25 seconds "advancing to pulsar" without moving. Merging into one 60-unit body (24 Bulls, 30 Stouts, ~30k metal) and then setting footwork to fan only for the attacking groups was the right move; I only found both at 29:53 and 31:22, with two minutes left on the clock. Do both the first time you point an army at a fortified position, and if a group's order says it is advancing while its position doesn't change for ten seconds, stop trusting the order and name a concrete ground spot to fight to instead.

Two smaller things. Never reuse a group or build label — my third advanced plant order was silently refused at 30:20 because #avp3 was taken, and that cost a turn. And his single tier-3 units (a Marauder, then two Razorbacks, ~3,800 metal each) walked out alone and killed six Shellshockers in twenty seconds; attacking them with the whole body was correct and cheap, but don't let that chase pull you off the nest, which is where I spent my last ninety seconds.

If I sat down again: run the same opening, make energy the explicit second priority from 8:00, and spend minutes 12-20 — when we already had a 4:1 army lead — building one consolidated body with fan footwork and killing the H2 bot lab, instead of grinding spot by spot against the turret line. The lead was never the problem.
