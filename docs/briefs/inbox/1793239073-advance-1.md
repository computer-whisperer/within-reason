# advance-1: the player's debrief

Match `run/matches/1793239073-advance-1/00`, build f5a4ad9de0e5, against BARb stable hard_aggressive on Comet Catcher Remake 1.8: Timeout after 17.7 min (called by the referee); 100 turns. Debriefed by claude-opus-5 (effort low) in 28 s, in a shape of its own (DECIDED / NEXT PLAYER / WISHED was asked); since snapshot: 7-day +0.0 points, extra usage +0.00.

We started at B8, the south-west corner; his base is the north-east, bot lab at G2, commander nest around F3-F4. The map's shape decided this game: his commander plus three Sentries and a Centurion sit at F3 astride the direct route, and his economy is north behind it. Do not walk a body into that nest — I did at 9:15 and it cost 1,540 metal in one minute, with a 7-second order lag between deciding to leave and leaving. Go round by the east (spot_49, 41, 42, 29, 34, 25, 17, 12, then his lab), keeping every stop 1,000+ away from the nest guns.

The opening worked. Armada: two extractors, two solars, vehicle plant at ~0:34, third extractor, commander assists the plant at low priority. Budget more energy than you think — I stalled at 1:20 and again at 2:00, and had to interrupt the commander for two solars each time; the plant at full speed draws ~240 e/s against 120 coming in. Put a Sentry in the same list as each outer extractor from the start. His opener is a bot lab: a Tick is inside your base by 2:10 and it is faster than your commander and your Blitzes, so chasing it is wasted minutes — I lost spot_74 and burned three turns on it. The answer is the Sentry at home, not the chase. After the Tick come Pawn packs of 6-9 which eat extractors and constructors behind your army all game; Blitzes catch Pawns, Stouts do not, so keep a standing Blitz home guard and never let it dissolve into the main body.

Economy ran well ahead of his: 10 extractors at 5:32, 15 at 7:45, 20 by 12:30 against his ~10, army 4x his at 7:45. That lead is the whole game and I never converted it. Three reasons, all avoidable. First, I spent 9:15-9:41 feeding soldiers into the F3 nest and reset to a worse position. Second, at 12:48 I had energy capped at 3,074 and flat metal with six builders idle — watch for the inverse stall and put constructors on free spots (B5, A4, C4, D6, E6, D5) and converters on the commander. Third, I let the commander build a fusion at high priority at 15:26 while extractors needed rebuilding; it starved the plants and my attempt to drop it to low priority was refused once and had to be resent without the solar step. Build the fusion at low priority from the first order.

His tier 2 arrived at 15:26 — a Hound at C4, then parties of two Hounds, a Gunslinger and Rocketeers, roughly 1,800-2,000 metal each. That is the clock: convert before 15:00 or you are playing his game. From 16:00 on I lost 18 extractors in three minutes to three or four such parties scattered across B4, D8, E7 while a 35-strong group_Q chased each one thirty seconds behind and never caught any. One big body cannot answer dispersed tier-2 raids. Split into several companies early enough that each can reach a raid, and keep each strong enough to beat 2,000 metal, because Hounds outrange Stouts and you must close rather than trade.

The ending is the lesson I most want to pass on. At 17:40 his party was inside our base at B7-B8 killing the advanced plant and the nanos, and group_Q — 43 soldiers, 8,940 metal, against about 2,000 — stood at A7 with two of forty-three in weapons reach. The block halts in ranks and the cramped base geometry means it never engages. I set company cap 8 to make small companies flood in, too late. If your body is fighting in your own base, cap companies small before the fight, not during it.

Things I would do differently in order: keep the body out of the F3 nest entirely and raid his northern spots instead; push the conversion attack to around 10:00-11:00 with everything, by the east route, while his field army is still ~2,000; hold a permanent Blitz home guard from 5:00; fusion and all big builds at low priority; small company caps before 15:00. The one rule I wrote that cost me was "every detachment forbidden while his army stands in our half" — it was right against the Pawn-pack era, but by 16:00 it meant one slow mass chasing four fast raids, and I should have replaced it the moment his tier 2 appeared.
