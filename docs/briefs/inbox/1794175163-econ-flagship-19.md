The real lesson of the game is the late loop, and you should get there earlier than I did. After the first fusion (8:29, 128/s) the economy becomes a three-way race: extractors → fusions → advanced converters, and the limit flips between "energy reaching the converters" and "converter capacity" every minute or two. I spent 13:00-18:00 chasing those flips one turn at a time. Watch the two numbers the report gives you — energy a second the converters could take versus energy a second they get — and when capacity exceeds supply put fusions first in turret priority, when supply exceeds capacity put converters first. Build both ahead of the flip rather than reacting; converters were stuck at 40 for a full minute of a 20-minute game purely because I was late.

The expensive mistake to avoid: I built a dense block of 60 turrets (12,000 build power) for assistance and it penned my own advanced constructors out of its frame slots — two of them walked for minutes (29164/20308), and sent-to-"block" converter builders wandered the perimeter (7477 went from 891 to 1,039 elmos to go). Stop turret laying once you have ~12,000 bp there, and place converters and advanced fusions at marks just *outside* the block but inside turret reach (I used conv_south, conv_west, conv_north) — not inside it. Also check walking distance before marking a site: conv_field was a 60-130 s walk and constructor_6660 was about to walk 4,200 elmos to a mark when nearby east mohos were free. Nearby work beats a better-shaped site every time at this tempo. Two basic converters also penned constructor_12458 and had to be reclaimed; keep lanes clear.

Several T1 constructors sharing the same list ids as an advanced constructor is how you get helpers onto a frame cheaply — I used that for mohos and for the fusion fields (one armacv placing, 5-6 T1s sharing ids). Phase 2 after the first fusion is advanced constructors on mohos at the home/north/south clusters. Nothing built in the last ~80 s pays back, so stop ordering around 18:40 and let frames finish.
