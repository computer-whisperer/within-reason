### The cost of missing the commander

**The heuristic.** Irishstud14, watching our commanders walk off their plants at 1:46: "removing your com from the lab
too early. you're missing out on the BP for the lab"; thebluegecko after the same series: the commander should be
assisting the lab when it first comes up. The plain line: from the moment the plant stands until the advanced plant
is wanted, the commander stands at the plant and guards it, and leaves only for what nothing else can do yet, the
near spots in the first minute and a solar it builds in its reach. Follow it whenever the metal store is rising.

**The quantity.** Constructors standing at 4:00, and behind them the plant's build power. The plant alone is 150; the
commander is 300, twice the plant and three and a third constructors, and it is the only build power the side owns
before the constructors exist. Every second of it goes somewhere, and the question of the opening is where. Not the
commander's own output: an extractor in six seconds and a solar in nine look like work, and the arithmetic below
says what that work displaces.

**The mechanism.** Three prices, all from the build-power fact.

- *What the plant does with it.* A construction vehicle is 4,050 of build on 135 metal. The plant alone makes one in
  27 seconds, every 29 in our games, and no bank changes that. With the commander guarding, the power on it is 450 and
  the constructor takes 9 seconds, if 15 metal a second is there to pay for it; below that income the metal is the
  ceiling and the constructor takes as long as the metal does. So the commander's assist buys nothing while the store
  is empty and falling (the pool's plants run 1.5 to 1.6 times faster under it, not 3.0, for this reason), and buys
  up to two constructors a minute while the store is rising. The store is the whole signal: metal banked beside a
  plant alone is build power short, and the build power that is short is standing somewhere else.
- *What the field does with it.* The commander walks 37.5 elmos a second and builds an extractor in 6; a spot 500
  away is 13 seconds each way and 6 at the spot, so the field trip yields one extractor per 30 to 45 seconds and
  nothing while it walks (in our game 11, three extractors and a solar in two minutes, 4,500 elmos of walking, two
  of every three seconds spent walking). A constructor does the same spot in 55 seconds all in (his nine, measured),
  walking faster than the commander (54 against 37.5) and building slower. So in the field the commander is worth
  one and a half constructors. At the plant, with metal in the bank, it is worth two constructors a minute, each of
  which is then worth a spot every 55 seconds for the rest of the game. The field trip pays once; the plant compounds.
- *What a solar costs, by who builds it.* The commander builds a solar in 8.7 seconds: 2,600 of build that would have
  been two thirds of a constructor at the plant, if metal allowed; a constructor builds it in 29 seconds, half an
  extractor at its 55. Which is cheaper depends on the store. Under 20 percent and falling, the commander's seconds at
  the plant are worth nothing extra and the solar is free: that is his pattern, a solar every thirty seconds beside
  the plant and guard between, the commander never more than its reach from the door. Rising, every solar the
  commander builds is two thirds of a constructor, and the constructor should build it. And a solar into a full
  energy store is wrong at any price: 155 metal taken from the constructor the plant is waiting on, for energy nowhere
  to put. The solar's right time is set by what the plant will draw: constructors cost 14.4 energy per metal, so a
  plant with the commander on it spending 15 metal a second draws 216 energy, the commander's 30 and nine solars;
  those solars are owed before the income reaches 15, in the plant's slack, not after, and not four at once at 1:14
  into a store at 99 percent while the store of metal reads 7.

**Three openings on one task.** Comet Catcher, the opponent inactive, the same start. His plant at 1:01; ours at 1:02
and 0:37. In the first minute all three commanders did the same work: three spots and two or three solars within 450
of the start, then the plant. The difference is minutes 2 to 5.

His commander left once, early, for spots the constructors had not reached, and came back to a plant whose metal was
tight the whole time (6 to 32 percent): the assist's value was in minute 3, when the income reached 20 and four
constructors came in 42 seconds. Ours left at 2:06 and 3:00 and did not come back until the advanced plant; the
store rose to 70 percent behind a plant making one constructor every 29 seconds. The bank's rise alone, about 500
metal, was three constructors not made by 4:30, each then a spot every 55 seconds: five extractors by 5:00 and
eight by 6:00 against the trip's three, and the gap growing. That is the whole of the 26 against 17.

**The advanced plant, the same mistake at a larger price.** Game 11's commander came home at 4:08 and built the
advanced plant alone, 122 seconds (90 nominal, the rest stalled), on seven solars and no storage: 14,000 energy over
two minutes is 115 a second on an income of 170, the store under 5 percent for 58 seconds, metal income flat from
5:00 to 6:00. His went up at 5:35 on 26 extractors, after an energy storage at 4:06, with three builders, in 43
seconds. The build-power table has the two clocks: 300 seconds for a constructor alone, 41 for the commander with four
constructors (660). Two minutes of the commander alone on a frame is two minutes of a plant making two constructors
instead of up to a dozen.

**Where it bends.** The first minute: the commander is the only builder, so the stand's spots and the first solars are
its work and nothing is displaced (on a metal map every tile is a spot and it builds the whole opening from where it
stands, K-eco-a-commander-at-its-stand-builds-from-where-it-stands). A spot nobody else can reach before a raider
would: the trip pays once, and once may be the moment. The store empty and falling with the plant running: the
commander's hands are free, and his free hands build solars in the plant's reach. A plant making soldiers rather than
constructors: the same accounting, the commander's 300 as a Blitz every 4.4 seconds instead of 13.3, and the metal
the ceiling likewise. And the opponent: against anyone active the commander is also the opening's gun, and where it
stands in minutes 3 to 6 is the raid article's question too; the inactive opponent is the one case where the
build-power answer is the whole answer.

**When to depart from the line.** When the metal store is empty and falling, for a solar or a near spot, and back.
When a raider is at a spot the commander can reach and nothing else can. When the plant is reclaimed for the advanced
plant and the commander is the frame's first builder: then with the constructors beside it, after the storage, not
alone on seven solars. Never because the spots are there and the commander is idle between constructors: idle beside
the plant with the store empty is the cheapest seat in the game, and the field trip is the dearest.

**The tests.** Three games on one task, above; unchecked over a pool, and the pool's duels say the same thing weaker
(the commander guards the plant 50 s at the median of the first four minutes, winners assist less than losers because
their store is emptier, M-pool-opening). The kill-test: a flagship game on the econ task in which the commander stays
within 260 of the plant from the plant's standing to the advanced plant, with guard orders between its solars, and does
not pass eight constructors by 4:00 and 24 extractors by 5:00; or one in which the commander's field trip in minutes 2
to 4 claims spots faster than the constructors it displaced would have. The sign to read in any game: the metal store
rising past 30 percent while the commander walks.
