### The late economy: when nothing is short

**The heuristic.** thebluegecko's three resources (L-gecko-three-resources): "Metal is the basic resource. Everything
grows from it. E is sort of like a luxury resource. It's the resource that grows out of having more metal and
spending it. BP is how fast you can spend." His line at 8:01 of a 3v1, with our stores rising: "metal store is
getting higher, making more build power, maybe cons, maybe construction turrets could help" (L-energy-nanos). The
user's recollection of the ladder (K-t2-post-moho-scaling): "one or two fusions is reasonable as a transition to
advanced fusion, and once there heavy investment in advanced converters is what lets players scale further." And
the 8v8 front's demand of its tech seat, "T2 plz" at 7:02 and "who needs T2?" at 14:20 (M-format-8v8). Read
together they say: once metal sits unspent, the game is about build power and energy, in that order, and the energy
becomes metal.

The duel's economy articles (D-metal-is-the-game, D-energy-second, D-tier2-sprint) are written for the regime in which
something is short: which builder is served first in a stall, energy dear, a factory before the turrets because the
factory also makes soldiers, a converter last while a spot stands free. All of it holds while something is short.
This article is for the regime after that, which the pool's duels never reach (they end at 12:39 at the median and
built no fusion in 80 sides, M-pool-closing, M-pool-energy-and-power) and which every long game reaches: the 8v8
tech seat from its fourth minute, a bonus seat from its first, the econ benchmark from about 6:00, and a duel on Comet
that lasts past 15:00.

**The quantity.** Two numbers. The bank's age: metal at the store's cap is income that bought nothing, and the store
has been at cap in every long game of ours (econ-flagship-1 to -4: 500 to 700 seconds of twenty minutes, 40,000 to
58,000 standing at the end; the Glitters 2v6 of 2026-10-25: at cap from 19:00 to 41:00). And the price of the next
metal a second, in metal and in seconds, which is what the bank should be buying. Not the income alone, and not the
pool's reference: the duel pool spends half its metal on soldiers from the second minute, so a side with no army to
field is far ahead of its medians at every mark while far behind what the same metal could have made.

**The mechanism.**

- *It begins when nothing is short, not at a clock.* The signs are in the stores (F-eco-store, D-energy-second's
  reading): metal rising with the energy store full is build power short; both full is nothing short, and the regime
  has begun. The clock differs by format: his econ game spent to zero from the first minute to the eighteenth and
  banked only in the last two (K-eco-pro-curve-against-a-passive-opponent); the 8v8 tech seat banks from the start
  because the side's metal flows to it (M-format-8v8: the top receiver, 2,965 median and 10,562 at p75 over a game);
  a +50 seat banks 900 to 1,060 in the first three minutes on a +0 opening (F-eco-multipliers; human-8v8-1). Ours: a
  rising store that the duel article reads as "a plant paid" (D-tier2-sprint item 3) and then reads no further.

- *Past the spots, income grows only by turning energy into metal.* Every held spot is upgraded first: a moho is
  +6 metal a second for 620, 104 metal per metal a second, half the price of anything below (K-t2-post-moho-scaling).
  Then the price list per metal a second: an advanced fusion with five advanced converters 224 (11,600 for +51.7,
  repaid in 3.7 minutes); a fusion with its 1.25 converters 296; a basic converter with the solars to feed it 540
  (F-eco-consequences). The converter takes only the energy standing above three quarters of the energy store
  (F-eco-conversion), so a converter never starves a builder, and "energy in balance" with converters standing is the
  converters at work, not a reason to build none: the balance is theirs. What they make is bounded by that energy,
  not by their number: the gadget feeds the most efficient first, so once the advanced converters' capacity meets the
  energy income (39 of them take 23,400 a second) a basic converter beside them converts nothing, a converter built
  against a metal float is a body, and the gadget never reads the metal store, so a full one is still fed and the
  metal is lost (K-eco-conversion-income-is-bounded-by-the-energy-above-the-line; econ-flagship-14: 114 basic
  converters beside 39 advanced on 23,270 of energy, the last 101 of them adding nothing). The energy a frame draws is
  energy not converted, so each metal a second spent on advanced converter frames reads as about a metal a second less
  income while it builds. From this point on, energy is the metal, and his "luxury resource" has become the basic
  one, and the question of a slow frame is the power on it, not the store: a frame rises at the power told onto it
  over its build time, so four advanced fusions placed at once under 50 turrets go at a quarter of the block's pace
  each, and only an empty store makes a frame slower than its power (econ-flagship-14, 15:08: 0.4-0.9 % a second
  under 9-23 turrets apiece with the energy store at 97 %, read as an energy shortage, and 104 solars ordered).

- *The loop closes and compounds.* Energy buys metal through the converter; metal and build power buy energy through
  the fusion; metal and energy buy build power through the turret. Each engine (one advanced fusion and its five
  converters) adds about 47 percent to a seat at 110 metal a second and repays itself in 224 seconds, so a seat that
  spends to zero doubles every few minutes until its build power or the map stops it (K-t2-post-moho-scaling). The
  duel's book has no page for this because the duel never gets here; the tech seat's whole game is this page.

- *Build power takes the form of what it builds.* Extractors are spread over the map and want builders that walk;
  the frames of this regime (fusions 3,350, advanced fusions 9,700, converters 380, the gantry) all stand in one
  place and want power that stands there. A construction turret helps any frame within 400 (K-mech-turrets-assist-
  in-radius), one at a time in your `tend` order (the highest-ranked kind in its reach takes the whole turret; the
  plants' products last: left to the game a turret takes the nearest thing under construction, and a running plant is
  always that; your hands lay the block out of every plant's reach; why turret frames come first in the default order
  is in the next bullet) and is the cheapest build power in metal, 1.15 a point against the advanced constructor's 1.9 to 2.0
  (F-eco-consequences); a frame placed among turrets builds at their sum. The clocks: a fusion 186 seconds on one
  advanced construction vehicle, 54 at 1,000 power (five turrets); an advanced fusion 539 seconds on two advanced
  constructors, 226 with four turrets beside them, 143 with eight (K-t2-build-power-for-a-project-follows-the-spend);
  in his econ game, under a block that reached 304 turrets, an advanced fusion stood in 38 to 60 seconds and an
  advanced converter in about 5 (the record, read 2026-10-30). The duel's clock for a fusion, "about three minutes",
  is the lone constructor's and nothing else.

- *Standing power is a spending rate, and the kind sets it.* A frame draws its metal cost times the power on it
  over its build time (F-eco-build), so one construction turret spends, every second it works: 6.2 metal on an
  advanced fusion, 12.4 on a fusion, 8.3 on an advanced extractor, 8.7 on another turret, 19 on an advanced plant,
  2.2 on an advanced converter; and in energy 44, 67, 103, 121, 104 and 120 (K-eco-standing-power-spends-by-the-
  frame). A block's spend is its power times the kind under it: 24 turrets on advanced fusion frames move 150 metal
  a second and no order among them moves more; 112 turrets move 700. So the bank's rise, divided by the kind's
  figure, is the turrets the block is short, and the block's energy draw at full work (24 turrets on fusions: 1,600
  a second) is the energy that has to stand before the power is bought, which is why the fusion comes before the
  converters that will feed on it. The measure that travels is turrets per metal a second of income. His econ game
  held one turret for every six, at 10:00, 15:00 and 20:00 alike (29 / 213, 112 / 651, 304 / 1,706): his block
  could put his whole income into advanced fusions at any minute. Ours held one for every twenty (econ-flagship-11:
  24 / 458, 45 / 958; econ-10 one for thirteen), and the bank was the difference. The bank is a lagging sign: it
  says the power was short a minute ago, and in this regime income triples every five minutes, so a block that
  matches the income now is a third of what the next five minutes want. Turret frames under the block stand in about
  a second and the block's growth is the share of its power on them: his kept about a tenth of itself on new
  turrets, 35 a minute at the end, and lost almost nothing on the frames for it. That is the reason turret frames
  come first in the hands' default `tend` order, and the reason moving them down costs the curve more than it
  costs the frame (econ-flagship-11 at 15:57: turret frames moved below converters and the fusions, 45 turrets at
  18:00 and none after).

- *The block is ground before it is power.* A turret helps what stands within 400 of it, a circle of about 500,000
  square elmos, and the power a frame gets is whatever stands inside that circle round it, so the block is an area
  and what stands on the area is the whole question. The pitch sets how many turrets a frame can see: touching, 48
  apart, 274 turrets fit within reach of a frame's centre; at 96 apart, the hands' default until the `block` tool's
  `dense`, 68. His block stood at 48, his frames saw 46 to 53 turrets when placed; ours at 96 in econ-flagship-12
  saw 17 to 33 of 128, 74 of 102 turrets had no frame within reach at 18:00, and the block's 18 frame slots were down
  to two. Anything else on the ground is turrets not standing and frames not in reach: a solar's footprint is two
  and three quarter turrets', and the seventeen solars he reclaimed at 13:44 to 14:41 stood where thirteen to twenty
  turret frames went a minute later (the base article has the lattice arithmetic; D-air-not-optional's dispersal is
  the price of the same ground against bombers). So a generator belongs on the block's ground only when it will be
  eaten for it, and the block's room is a quantity to read beside its power: slots free, turrets in reach of a slot.

- *Who starts the frames pays the walk, not the build.* Under `start` a builder places a frame and leaves it the
  moment it stands up, about a second, so a starter's rate is frames a minute and its cost is the walk between them.
  A construction vehicle walks 54 a second and goes round a block, not through it: at 48 pitch nothing passes
  between the turrets, at 96 a vehicle fits the street. In econ-flagship-11 and -12 the constructors spent 54 and 43
  percent of their seconds walking (the audits), the advanced constructors 1,100 to 1,400 elmos a minute from 13:00
  to 19:00, a third to a half of every minute. The construction aircraft (110 metal, 2,100 energy, 50 of build, 136 a
  second, 156 health) flies over the block at two and a half times the vehicle's pace, and its 50 of build is beside
  the point for a starter; the advanced one (340 metal, 12,000 energy, 120 of build, 192 a second) starts the
  advanced frames. That is what his 46 construction aircraft were: from an air plant at 10:27 they placed 276 turrets,
  30 advanced fusions and 116 advanced converters inside the block from 13:23, the turrets finishing each. The
  trade: the flyer costs energy above all (19 energy a metal; his 46 were 97,000 energy, half a minute of his energy
  income at 15:00) and dies to anything (a Thistle or a fighter kills it in a pass, and it leaves no wreck), so it is
  the starter only where the air over the block is ours or nobody's; a ground constructor walks slower and lives.
  The sign that the starter is the limit, not the power: frame slots free and turrets in reach of them idle while the
  constructors are walking, which is the block waiting for frames. Before that the flyer is 110 metal and 2,100 energy
  for nothing, and under his air it is a gift.

- *The turret's energy column is paid once a fusion stands (ours).* D-energy-second prices the turret by its 3,200
  energy, "five and a third minutes of one solar", and that is why the duel builds one and then a factory. Beside a
  standing fusion the same 3,200 is four seconds of its 750; beside an advanced fusion, one second (F-unit-armfus,
  F-unit-armafus). The metal column, 230, is then the whole price, and it is the lowest on the list. The rule "a
  factory before the turrets" is a scarcity rule and ends with the scarcity.

- *Power beyond the spend only stalls, so the block's size follows the spend.* A frame can use build time times
  the metal a second you give it over its metal cost, and more stands idle (K-t2-build-power-for-a-project-follows-
  the-spend): a fusion at 50 metal a second uses 810 power, at 400 it uses 6,400 (32 turrets), at 900 it uses 14,500.
  A bank raises the spend above the income for as long as it lasts: 45,000 banked is 196 turrets, 13 fusions or
  four and a half advanced fusions, and it can be spent at any rate the power on the frames allows. So the block is
  sized to the income and the bank together, and a bank with a small block is the one state the regime forbids.

- *Each tier's infrastructure is eaten for the next.* The plant by the commander when its constructors are out, the
  advanced plant by the turrets when its constructors are out (seven seconds under them in his game), the solars
  where the block is to go once the fusions carry the energy; a reclaimed unit returns its metal whole in the second
  the reclaim ends and none of its energy (F-eco-reclaim, K-t2-people-eat-each-plant-for-the-next, K-eco-pro-curve-
  against-a-passive-opponent: 7,200 reclaimed before 20:00; the Center Command sprint, M-gecko-team: each advanced
  plant eaten for the next, an advanced air plant standing in four seconds with 36 turrets within 300). The plant is
  a loan, not a possession (D-tier2-sprint item 7).

- *The field is one target.* A block of turrets round a plant is one bomber pass's target with its whole build power
  (D-air-not-optional, its rule 3: turrets 48 apart or in small groups, converters 60 apart); a field of fusions and
  converters is the nuke's target (D-surprise-weapons). The dispersal and the anti-air are the regime's running
  cost, and the duel article has them.

- *What the game demands now bounds the share (the knob).* An economy spent wholly on itself doubles fastest and
  fields nothing; every game with an adversary takes a share of the income for the army, the turrets and the raids
  before the loop gets the rest, and that share is what the pool's medians have paid. The inputs to it: his army
  against ours and where it stands (D-metal-is-the-game: a raid on our extractors is answered first); who stands in
  front of us (K-t2-the-gap-is-covered-by-allies: a seat with allies holding the fronts can set the share near zero,
  as the tech seat does to 16:00); and the game's horizon (a duel that ends by 12:39 never repays an advanced fusion;
  an 8v8 at 40 minutes is mostly this regime). The share is a judgment from these three, not a number, and the
  instances below are the same loop at different settings of it.

- *In a team the loop is one seat's and the side's metal flows to it.* The tech seat receives the side's metal and
  returns advanced constructors and later units (M-format-8v8: lane 5 the top receiver in 13 of 33 lost sides, 10
  units given a game, "all front should have t2 now"; K-team-two-seats-feed-one-seats-tier-2); the energy is the
  seat's own unless shared, so the tech seat carries the side's energy bill and the front seats need none of it. A
  seat of ours receiving overflow it does not read is the Glitters 2v6: metal at cap from 19:00 with the allies'
  overflow of 30 to 47 a second unread and the economy line saying "in balance" and "FULL" together.

- *A bonus sets the knob early and makes the loop the best buy from the first minute.* The room's handicap multiplies
  every income including a converter's output, so a +50 seat makes 2.25 times an unbonused seat's metal from the
  same generators (F-eco-multipliers); the regime begins at 0:00 because the +0 opening cannot spend the income, and
  the price list above is read with conversion at 1 over 2.25 of its +0 price. BARb at +20 to +50 is this regime
  played against us: a level game to about 20:00, then the bonus income, a static line and the gantry past 25:00,
  while we stood in front of it with a lead of two to ten times his army and read ourselves as ahead (M-barb-bonus).
  The AI tech seat at +65 is its clean form: the advanced lab at 2:31 to 2:54, turrets from 2:56 to 4:29, 69 to 179
  of them a game, 171 converters, no army to 16:00 (M-format-8v8).

**Consider, and why it may work.**

1. *Read a bank as frames not begun, and ask what stands fastest.* Why: the regime's price is in seconds, not metal;
   a frame that stands in 50 seconds instead of 190 is 140 seconds of its output, and the bank is already paid. The
   income article's question, what is cheapest in metal, has no answer worth having when metal is at cap.
2. *Buy build power with the bank, in its cheapest form, where the frames will stand.* Why: 1.15 metal a point and
   the energy column paid once a fusion runs; a frame placed among turrets builds at their sum, so the same bank buys
   the frame and its clock together. A second advanced plant and forty advanced constructors is the same purchase at
   1.9 a point, each needing the plant's 600 of power to be born, each walking to its frame alone. And buy it ahead
   of the bank, sized to the income the next five minutes will bring: a turret bought while the store is flat is
   spend capacity for the rest of the game, one bought after the store caps is yesterday's shortfall, and the bank's
   rise divided by six is the turrets the block was already short.
3. *Upgrade every spot first, then the ladder, and converters with the fusions, not after them.* Why: 104 metal per
   metal a second against 224 and 296, so the spots come first while any stands free; and a fusion without its
   converters is energy at cap, which is a bill paid and nothing bought (the Altored +50 3v3: a seat banked 2,000
   energy a second unused for eight minutes with one converter). The converter takes only the energy above three
   quarters of the store, so it is never the reason a builder stalls.
4. *Set the share by the knob and say which setting it is.* Why: the instances below span zero (his econ game) to
   most (the duel), and the right one follows from his army, who stands in front, and the horizon. A side that
   names its setting can tell whether it is behind the uncapped curve or ahead of the duel's, and they are different
   questions.
5. *Eat the last tier's plant when its constructors are out.* Why: 2,600 back in seven seconds under the turrets, a
   fusion's worth, and the plant's storage and lane are not needed once the block builds the frames.
6. *In a team, send what the loop cannot spend and read what arrives.* Why: metal at cap in a receiving seat is the
   side's metal lost; the sending seats' overflow is income the seat has to count or it reads itself as level.
7. *Disperse the field as the air article says.* Why: the block is the one thing of ours worth a bomber wing or a
   missile, and it is where all the build power stands.

**The ways the discipline fails.** *The float:* the store at cap with the income
unspent. *The lone clock:* frames begun with no standing power in reach, each on one or two walking builders. *The
wrong curve:* reading the side as ahead against the duel's medians while a passive opponent or a fed seat puts it on
the uncapped curve. *The withheld converter:* converters refused because energy is "in balance" while fusions stand.
*The frozen power:* no builder of any kind begun for minutes while the store rises. *The flat block:* the turret count
standing still while the income doubles, the block's spend a fixed fraction of a rising income.

**Where it bends.** (1) The duel does not reach it: a Comet duel ends at 12:39 at the median and an advanced fusion
repays in 224 seconds only if the game lasts them; there D-tier2-sprint's gate holds and this article is for the
game that outlives it. (2) A metal map cuts the loop (D-metal-maps): with metal at cap from the first minute and no
spots to run out of, conversion buys nothing and the regime is build power and energy alone. (3) A bonus moves the
beginning to 0:00 and makes conversion the best buy per generator; the share is still set by the knob. (4) While no
fusion stands the energy column is real and the duel's order holds: one turret on the plant, then the factory; the
flip is conditional on the energy being paid, not on the clock. (5) Who starts the block's frames is a choice of its own, and the mechanism above has the trade: his econ game used
construction aircraft under an empty sky; the Center Command sprint, under two people's armies, used tier-1
constructors and turrets finishing frames the advanced constructors began
(K-t2-moho-frames-finished-by-tier-1-build-power). (6) On few spots (the tech seat's six, Great Divide's eleven a
side) the mohos are done early and the loop is the whole income; on Comet's 39 a side the spots carry the growth to
about 15:00 and the loop takes over after. (7) The pool's medians are the right reference for a duel and the wrong
one for every other setting of the knob: the comparator is the instance with the same setting. (8) A Legion seat
(F-eco-legion): the ladder costs the same per metal a second in larger pieces (the fusion 950 for 4,000, the advanced
fusion 3,300 for 10,500, the fusion frame 244 seconds on one advanced construction vehicle against 186), so each step
wants more power standing beside it; the standing power can be the Fortifiers at the spots, 400 each, as well as a
block; a vent takes the Rampart (600 a second, an anti-nuke, three heavy drones) in place of a hardened geothermal,
which is the field's anti-nuke placed for it; and a Hive is an energy sink that stands beside the converters, 50
energy and 1.5 metal a second while it restocks six drones at 15 metal and 500 energy each.

**When to depart from the line.** When his army is at the field or our extractors with nothing answering: the raid
answer first, as in every article. When the kill is in sight within the frame's payback: the surplus goes into the
kill. When a spot stands free or un-upgraded: it comes before any converter. When energy is short and no fusion
stands: the duel's order, a factory before the turrets, and the generator read off the next draw (D-energy-second).
When a bomber wing or a nuke is his: the field's dispersal and anti-air before its growth.

**The tests.** Not run. The cheap kill-test: over every recorded game of ours of sixteen minutes or more, the seconds
the metal store stood at its cap between 10:00 and 20:00 against income at 20:00 and the result; had the float been
harmless, the games with a long float would reach the same income as those without, and with a bonus or a passive
opponent the uncapped curve would not be reachable by anyone. What exists: his econ game and ours on the same task
read with `run/eco_marks.py --shape` (the instances above); the Glitters 2v6 and the Altored +50 3v3 as ledger rows
and a card. What the test cannot do: the pool has no long game to compare, so the uncapped curve rests on one game
of his and the tech seats' counts, and a second game of his or another strong player's on the task is the first
thing that would move this article.
