We win by killing the enemy commander and lose when ours dies. Extractors on metal spots are the income, income becomes soldiers, and the bigger army wins; energy only needs to keep the store from emptying. The opponent raids builders and extractors with small fast squads from the first minutes and sends its army as one block later. Fights are decided by the metal of soldiers on the spot, a turret counting three times its metal. A raider at one of our extractors is met by a small detachment (send_against), not the whole group. A group advancing (fight_to) fights through everything on the way and there; a group holding or engaging steps out of turret reach on its own; a group moving without fighting runs from everything. "continue" keeps an actor's task; choose something else only when it is clearly better. Places are named: home, spot_N, passage_N and the player's own names; where the enemy is comes only from what our units have seen (its factories and commander as last seen, the spots nobody of ours has looked at); `shelling` is only where a weapon hitting us from out of sight likeliest stands; a group advances onto it only when the instructions say so, and otherwise goes to the place the instructions name. The words beside the numbers say what matters. An enemy party in sight within 600 of a group that outweighs it is fought now, whatever the instructions call it. The instructions were written before what the picture shows now. A group whose own `enemies_near` line says the party outweighs it, or whose `losses` line says it is losing this fight or being wiped out, falls back (fall_back) to where it last held, or to the place the instructions name for that case, whatever they said about keeping on: they could not see this. Losing a few soldiers, or a noticeable share, against a party it outweighs is the cost of fighting and no reason to fall back, even while that party is killing some of them. A group already walking back, to where it last held or home, carries on with its walk. An instruction that reached the hands after the group's last loss (the `player` line says when its last orders arrived, the `losses` line when the last loss was) was written with that loss in view and stands over this rule. The `player` line says when the player is out and cannot yet have seen this. The commander's `enemies_near` line weighs it as the fighter it is, not by its metal: it fights what comes to it and raiders that must walk into its D-gun, and it never walks toward, builds toward or attacks a party that outreaches its D-gun or outweighs it; away from home it walks back from such a party (retreat_home, or walk_to a place of ours behind it) before it is hit, whatever it was building.
