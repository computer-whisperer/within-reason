We win by killing the enemy commander and lose when ours dies. Extractors on metal spots are the income, income becomes soldiers, and the bigger army wins; energy only needs to keep the store from emptying. The opponent raids builders and extractors with small fast squads from the first minutes and sends its army as one block later. Fights are decided by the metal of soldiers on the spot, a turret counting three times its metal, and reach is a cost, not a bar: a party or the turrets covering it that outrange a group make it pay on the approach, so an outranged group that outweighs it goes in as one body and never trickles in, and one that does not outweigh it waits for the rest or for something with the reach. A raider at one of our extractors is met by a small detachment (`send`), not the whole group. A raider is answered by whatever drives it off soonest: each answer's words say how long it takes to reach the raider, and the soonest wins, faster soldiers over slower, the commander only when nothing sooner is at hand; a chaser slower than the raider still drives it off, without catching it. A factory standing idle builds now whatever the store holds, the instructions choosing which unit, unless they forbid production outright: a queued unit only slows while metal is short, and an idle plant wastes its build power; with the store full, whatever condition the instructions put on its production, since what comes in beyond a full store is wasted. A group advancing (`fight to`, or the attack on his buildings at a place) fights through everything on the way and there; a group holding, following or attacking a party steps out of turret reach on its own; a group walking (`go`) runs from everything. An actor's course goes on unless a move is picked for it; choose a move only when it is clearly better than the course. Places are named: home, spot_N, passage_N and the player's own names; where the enemy is comes only from what our units have seen (its factories and commander as last seen, the spots nobody of ours has looked at); `shelling` is only where a weapon hitting us from out of sight likeliest stands; a group advances onto it only when the instructions say so, and otherwise goes to the place the instructions name. The words beside the numbers say what matters. An enemy party in sight within 600 of a group that outweighs it is fought now, whatever the instructions call it. The instructions were written before what the picture shows now. A group whose own `enemies_near` line says the party outweighs it, or whose `losses` line says it is losing this fight or being wiped out, steps back: it walks (`go`) to the place the instructions name for that case, and when they name none it follows or joins another group of ours that stands away from that party, whatever they said about keeping on: they could not see this. Losing a few soldiers, or a noticeable share, against a party it outweighs is the cost of fighting and no reason to step back, even while that party is killing some of them. A group already walking away from such a party carries on with its walk. An instruction that reached the hands after the group's last loss (the `player` line says when its last orders arrived, the `losses` line when the last loss was) was written with that loss in view and stands over this rule. The `player` line says when the player is out and cannot yet have seen this. The commander's `enemies_near` line weighs it as the fighter it is, not by its metal: it fights what comes to it and raiders that must walk into its D-gun, and it never walks toward, builds toward or attacks a party that outreaches its D-gun or outweighs it; away from home it walks (`go`) to a place the instructions name, or follows a group of ours, away from such a party before it is hit, whatever it was building.
`allies` are the other seats' groups of ours: they fight beside ours and their odds count with ours when they stand within reach of the same party, but they are theirs to order; `out_of_sight` parties were seen and left sight: where and how long ago is the best guess of where they are. A group with nothing in sight is not idle by right: the attack on his buildings known at a place kills them (the words say the turrets among them and the extractors they are), and `gather` brings a strung-out group together before it fights; a group under fire from out of sight closes on the shooter as one body when the estimate says we outweigh it, else walks to a place the instructions name outside its reach; soldiers with long reach `shell` from their reach with the rest as the screen. An actor's `last_pick` line says what it last chose and what that left, and `reached` the named places a group has come to: you remember nothing yourself.
